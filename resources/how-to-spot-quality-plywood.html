7 Visual Checks to Spot Defects on Site.

How to check panel flatness, examine edge cross-sections for core voids, test veneer bonding, and verify genuine calibration before cutting begins.

Zero Core Voids. True Gauge.

Practical on-site verification methods.

1. Inspect the Edges for Core Gaps

Look closely at the cut edge of the sheet. High quality plywood shows crisp, solid, parallel veneer lines with zero hollow gaps or overlapping plies. Core gaps lead to weak spots where screws pull loose.

2. Measure Thickness at All 4 Corners (Calibration Check)

Use a digital vernier caliper to check thickness at all four corners and the center. A calibrated sheet like Umeka maintains uniform thickness across the board, avoiding uneven shutter faces.

3. The Tap Test (Acoustic Void Inspection)

Tap across the center and faces of the sheet with your knuckles or a small wooden block. A solid, uniform sound indicates good glue bonding. A dull, hollow thump reveals hidden internal delamination or voids.

4. Check Full-Sheet Planar Flatness

Stand the sheet on its edge or lay it flat on the floor. Quality plywood should not show severe diagonal warping, bowing, or twisting.

5. Surface Smoothness & Grain Texture

Run your palm over the face veneer. It should feel completely smooth, free from rough splinters, raised grain ripples, or rough patches that could telegraph through thin laminate sheets.

6. Weight & Core Density

Lift the sheet. Dense hardwood veneers with high solid resin content feel substantial. Very light sheets usually indicate low-density filler woods or hollow core layers.

7. Clear Brand & Grade Markings

Check the factory stamp. Quality manufacturers clearly brand their sheets with brand name, grade (MR/BWR/BWP), batch number, and manufacturing location.
