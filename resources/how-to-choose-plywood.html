How to Choose the Right Plywood Grade.

A practical, objective guide on matching thickness, moisture grades, and calibration standards to your specific joinery application.

Objective Material Guidance.

Written by experienced timber specialists.

1. Identify the Room & Moisture Exposure

Not every piece of furniture needs waterproof marine grade ply, but choosing non-waterproof boards in wet areas is a common and costly mistake:

  • Dry Areas (Bedrooms, Wardrobes, TV Units): Moisture-Resistant (MR) or standard calibrated commercial ply is ideal.
  • High-Humidity Zones (Kitchens, Dining, Bath Vanity Backdrops): Boiling Water Resistant (BWR) or Boiling Water Proof (BWP / Marine Grade conforming to IS 710) is essential to prevent edge swelling.

2. Choose the Right Panel Thickness

Selecting the proper thickness ensures structural support without over-spending on unnecessary weight:

  • 6mm / 9mm: Drawer bottoms, cabinet backings, and curved aesthetic panelling.
  • 12mm: Internal partitions, small box framing, and drawer sides.
  • 16mm / 18mm / 19mm: Main cabinet carcasses, wardrobe shutters, kitchen carcasses, and bed boxes.
  • 25mm: Heavy load-bearing workbench tops and thick structural shelving.

3. Always Opt for Calibrated Plywood

Uncalibrated plywood often varies in thickness by up to 2mm between corners. When carpenters join panels, this leads to uneven steps that require planing down on site. Calibrated plywood passes through industrial thickness sanders, ensuring that every edge is exactly the same gauge for flawless zero-gap modular assembly.
