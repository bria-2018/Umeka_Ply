    Frequently Answered Questions

    Plain-language explanations to help you choose the right material for your application.

    What is the core difference between calibrated plywood and block board? +

    Plywood is composed of cross-laminated thin timber veneers with alternating 90° grain orientations, delivering uniform strength and high screw retention in both directions (ideal for cabinet carcasses, wardrobes, and kitchens). Block board contains a core of solid wood batten strips, giving high resistance to bending along long horizontal spans (ideal for dining tables, conference desks, and wide shelves).

    What does "calibrated" mean and why does it matter on site? +

    Calibrated plywood is sanded using heavy-duty wide-belt calibration machines to a uniform thickness standard. Conventional uncalibrated plywood often fluctuates significantly in gauge within a single sheet. Calibration eliminates uneven steps when joining panels, prevents wavy laminate telegraphing, and ensures modular shutters hang perfectly parallel.

    Which plywood grade is recommended for modular kitchens and bathrooms? +

    For wet areas like kitchen sink units and bathroom vanity cabinets, choose Boiling Water Proof (BWP) grade bonded with synthetic resin. For dry areas such as bedroom wardrobes and living room storage, Moisture Resistant (MR/BWR) grade provides dependable performance.

    How does Umeka support architects and interior designers? +

    We provide curated physical sample boxes, standardized tender specification clauses for drawing legends, laboratory test report datasheets, and direct architectural coordination for custom sizes and bulk project procurement.
